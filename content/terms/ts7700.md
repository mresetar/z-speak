To z/OS, a TS7700 is a tape library with 3490-type drives, attached by FICON. A job mounts a volume, writes it and unloads it, as tape jobs always have. Inside, there is no tape in the path: the "volume" is a file in a disk cache, and a mount takes a second instead of a minute. Tens of thousands of virtual volumes can exist, and far more virtual drives than a real library would have.

What happens next depends on the model. Disk-only systems keep everything in cache. Others migrate volumes that are no longer in use to physical **3592** cartridges in an attached library, or to cloud object storage, and recall them on demand.

Its defining feature is the **grid**. Up to eight clusters, usually in different data centres, are connected over IP and present themselves as one library. Each volume is copied between clusters according to policy: synchronously, at the moment the volume is unloaded, or deferred. Any cluster can mount any volume, so the loss of a site leaves the tape data available at another.

Those policies are selected through z/OS. The names of the SMS **storage class**, **management class**, **data class** and **storage group** assigned to a tape allocation are passed with the mount, and the TS7700 looks up what each name means in its own definitions — so tape behaviour is governed by the installation's ACS routines.
