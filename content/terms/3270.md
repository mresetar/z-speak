A 3270 is not a character-at-a-time terminal. The host sends a formatted screen with protected and unprotected fields; you type into it locally; only when you press an **AID** key (Enter, a PF key, Clear) does the terminal transmit the changed fields back. One network round trip per screen instead of per keystroke — which is why the protocol scaled to tens of thousands of users on 1970s networks, and why it still feels instant today.

Physical 3270s are long gone. What you use is a **TN3270 emulator** (IBM PCOMM, x3270, Vista, or a browser-based one) speaking the 3270 data stream over TCP/IP. The habits it leaves behind are visible everywhere: PF-key legends at the bottom of a screen, fixed 24×80 layouts, and the word "panel" for what other people call a page.
